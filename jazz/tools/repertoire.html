Repertoire & listening

The standards every jazz guitarist is expected to know, grouped by the skills they teach, plus the recordings that define the style.

The course never prints copyrighted melodies. Learn tunes from recordings (best), from a legally published fake book (the Hal Leonard Real Book volumes), or from iReal Pro chord charts. "Knowing a tune" means: melody from memory, chords from memory in the original key, the form (number of bars and sections), and the ability to start it at a session without a chart.

How to learn a tune (the method used from Module 12 on)

  1. Listen to three recordings; pick one as your reference.
  2. Sing the melody with the record until you have it from memory.
  3. Find the melody on the guitar by ear, in the record's key.
  4. Work out the bass notes by ear, then the chord qualities; compare with a chart only afterwards.
  5. Write the form on an index card: sections, bar counts, key centres.
  6. Play melody, then chords, then melody with bass notes, then improvise, with a metronome on 2 and 4.
  7. Play it in a second key a week later. Keep a list; review two old tunes every practice day.
