How to practise ear training

  1. Start narrow. Two or three items at a time (for example major 3rd vs. minor 3rd vs. perfect 5th). Add one item when you hit 90 % over twenty questions.
  2. Sing before you answer. Sing the lower note, then the upper note; for chords, sing the root and then arpeggiate. The voice is what connects the ear to the fretboard.
  3. Use reference songs for intervals (the first two notes of a melody you know), but aim to outgrow them: eventually the interval should simply sound like itself.
  4. Chord qualities matter more than intervals for jazz. Being able to hear maj7 vs. 7 vs. m7 vs. m7b5 on the bandstand is a professional skill.
  5. Dominant colours (7, 7b9, 7#9, 7alt, 13, 7#11) are the sounds you will choose between when comping and soloing; learn to hear them.
  6. Do five minutes a day, not an hour on Sunday.
