Glossary

Jazz and guitar terms used in the course.

Altered dominant (7alt)
A dominant 7th chord whose 5th and 9th are both altered: b9, #9, #11 (b5) and b13 (#5) are available; the natural 5 and 9 are not. Played with the altered scale (7th mode of melodic minor).
Anticipation
Playing a chord or note an eighth note before the beat it belongs to (the "and of 4"). The core of swing comping rhythm.
Approach note
A note a half step or scale step away from a target chord tone, played just before it. Chromatic approach from below, diatonic approach from above, and enclosures (both) are bebop staples.
Arpeggio
The notes of a chord played one at a time.
Available tension
An extension (9, 11, 13 and their alterations) that can be added to a chord without clashing with its function: 9 and 13 on a dominant; 9, #11 and 13 on a major 7th; 9, 11, 13 on a minor 7th; 9 and 11 on a half-diminished.
Backcycling
Approaching a chord through a chain of dominants or ii–Vs a 5th apart (e.g. E7 A7 D7 G7 C).
Bebop scale
An eight-note scale with one chromatic passing tone added so that chord tones fall on downbeats when played in eighth notes: Mixolydian + natural 7 (bebop dominant), major + #5 (bebop major), Dorian + natural 3 (bebop Dorian).
Blue note
The b3, b5 and b7 played against a major or dominant chord.
Changes
The chord progression of a tune. "Rhythm changes" are the chords of Gershwin's I Got Rhythm.
Chord melody
A solo-guitar arrangement in which the melody is harmonised with chords beneath it.
Chord-scale
A scale chosen to match a chord so that every chord tone is in the scale and the remaining notes are available tensions.
Chord tone
A note that belongs to the chord: root, 3rd, 5th, 7th (and, loosely, 6th).
Comping
Accompanying: playing chords behind a soloist or singer with rhythmic interest.
Contrafact
A new melody written over the changes of an existing tune (e.g. Oleo over rhythm changes, Donna Lee over Indiana).
Diatonic
Belonging to the key's scale. Diatonic chords are built on each scale degree from scale notes only.
Diminished passing chord
A dim7 chord inserted between two diatonic chords a whole step apart (e.g. C – C#dim7 – Dm7), connecting the bass chromatically.
Drop-2 / drop-3 voicing
A four-note voicing made from a close-position chord by dropping the 2nd (or 3rd) voice from the top down an octave. The most-used jazz guitar chord shapes.
Enclosure
Surrounding a target note with notes above and below it before landing on it.
Extension
Notes beyond the 7th: 9, 11, 13 (the 2, 4, 6 an octave up).
Form
The structure of a tune in bars and sections: 12-bar blues, 32-bar AABA, ABAC, 16-bar, through-composed.
Guide tones
The 3rd and 7th of a chord: the two notes that define its quality and lead most smoothly to the next chord.
Head
The melody of a tune, played at the start and end of a performance; solos happen between.
ii–V–I
The fundamental jazz cadence: supertonic minor 7th, dominant 7th, tonic. In minor: iiø7 – V7(b9) – i.
Inversion
A chord with a note other than the root in the bass: 1st inversion (3rd in bass), 2nd (5th), 3rd (7th).
Modal interchange (borrowed chord)
Using a chord from the parallel minor (or another parallel mode) in a major key: iv, bVI, bVII, iiø7.
Mode
A scale built from the notes of a parent scale starting on a different degree: Dorian is the major scale from its 2nd degree.
Pedal point
A sustained or repeated bass note (usually the tonic or dominant) under changing chords.
Quartal harmony
Chords built in 4ths rather than 3rds. The sound of McCoy Tyner and of So What.
Rootless voicing
A chord voicing that omits the root (left to the bass player), typically 3-5-7-9 or 3-13-7-9 forms.
Secondary dominant
A dominant 7th chord that resolves to a diatonic chord other than I: V7/ii, V7/V, etc.
Shell voicing
A three-note voicing: root, 3rd and 7th (often root, 7th, 3rd low to high). Freddie Green's rhythm style uses these on the low strings.
Side-slipping
Playing a phrase a half step away from the key for tension, then returning.
Swing eighths
Eighth notes played with the first longer than the second, roughly in a 2:1 (triplet) ratio at medium tempo, becoming more even as the tempo rises.
Tritone substitution
Replacing a dominant 7th with the dominant 7th a tritone away (G7 → Db7); the two chords share the same 3rd and 7th.
Turnaround
A short progression (usually the last two bars of a section) leading back to the top: I – vi – ii – V, or iii – VI7 – ii – V, or I – bIII7 – bVI7 – bII7.
Upper-structure triad
A major or minor triad played over a dominant 7th's 3rd and 7th to produce specific tensions: e.g. an A triad over G7 (9, #11, 13), an Ab triad over G7 (b9, 3, b13).
Voice leading
Moving each note of a chord to the nearest note of the next chord; the measure of a well-connected chord progression.
Walking bass
A bass line in quarter notes that outlines the harmony with chord tones and approach notes; on guitar, the foundation of solo-guitar comping.
